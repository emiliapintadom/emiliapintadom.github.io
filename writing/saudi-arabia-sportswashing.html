How Saudi Arabia Uses Sportswashing to Promote or Hide Its Political Agenda

Emilia Pintado Mariscal 2024 25 min read

In the current international arena, politics and sports have been shown to interconnect. The importance of sports, nowadays, has evolved from simple entertainment. Now, cultural differences, political agendas and international relations all take part in the sports world. In order to take advantage of sports and use them as a political tool, countries have developed a technique called “sportswashing”.  This term alludes to the use of mass sporting events and investments to enhance a country’s international image, reputation, or to increase their soft power. Also, countries with a bad human right’s record tend to use sportswashing to divert public attention from their illegal practices. A clear example of how this strategy is implemented is Saudi Arabia, they often use sports to promote their political agenda or hide accusations regarding human rights.

Throughout this paper, I wish to explore the ways in which Saudi Arabia has participated in sportswashing practices. The research question is the following: How does Saudi Arabia use sportswashing to promote or hide political agendas? Initial results suggest that Saudi Arabia hosts massive international sporting events, invests in sports clubs around the world as well as sports infrastructure, and uses the manipulation of media campaigns to favor their political agenda.

Both academics and policymakers benefit from the research question I have presented. For academics, studying this subject, allows them to comprehend how the interconnection of politics and sports can have a negative impact on our society. It can begin a conversation on the ethical and moral implications of government’s involvement in sports. Diving into this study can help researchers understand the implications of sportswashing for the international community, while promoting the defense of human rights and fairness in sports. On the other hand, for policymakers, understanding how sportswashing works can provide answers on the use of sports as a mechanism of soft power. It can help formulate solutions on how to stop sports from manipulating international relations and public opinion.

Sportswashing is a current political strategy with the capacity to impact policymaking decisions and manipulate the masses, the importance of comprehending the tactic goes beyond simple curiosity. To prevent our society from falling victim to this political method, it is important to be informed. Also, policymakers can take better decisions when they are aware of the impact this technique can have on society. Once they are informed, they can develop appropriate strategies that set ethical boundaries while still promoting the beneficial aspects of sports. By studying this topic, we are contributing to a deeper understanding of the impact of sports in politics, and its effect on international relations.

The goal of this essay is to thoroughly break down Saudi Arabia's sportswashing tactic. First, the paper covers the background knowledge required to comprehend the idea of sportswashing, its history, and its application in the contemporary world. Then, the study dives more specifically into Saudi Arabia’s strategies. It examines how Saudi Arabia puts together enormous sporting events that attract spectators from all around the world. It delves further into how they choose which events to hold, how much money is involved in making them happen, and what their goals are by hosting them. Further on, the paper then dives into how Saudi Arabia finances international sports teams and constructs high-end sporting facilities. In this paper, I aim to provide explanations regarding the reasoning for these investments and their impact on Saudi Arabia's global reputation.

The study goes on to examine how Saudi Arabia modifies media coverage of sports in their favor. It explores how public perception can be manipulated by controlling the media narrative. Understanding the effects of Saudi Arabia's sportswashing, both domestically and globally, including the accusations they face from human rights organizations, is essential to the development of this research. Another objective during this paper is to investigate how Saudi Arabia's tactics of “sportswashing” have impacted diplomatic ties and changed the country's image abroad.

A discussion of the ethical issues of sportswashing is found at the conclusion of the paper. It explores how combining politics and sports detracts from the core values and goals of athletics. The research's conclusion also discusses the potential global implications of other nations ultimately implementing Saudi Arabia's policies. All of this is intended to improve public understanding of Saudi Arabia's sportswashing tactic, which will help researchers, policymakers, and sports enthusiasts alike. This study adds to the crucial discussion of the limitations and boundaries that should be established when integrating politics, sports, and ethics.

It is essential to start with the definition of sportswashing in order to comprehend how Saudi Arabia uses it as a political tactic. The term “sportswashing” describes the practice of governments using athletics to enhance their public image or divert attention from their controversial policies. According to a research paper published by Doctor Jules Boykoff, “Sportswashers use mega-events to try to foment national prestige and to convey economic or political advancement, the goal is to associate themselves with the positive images and emotions that sports can evoke, thereby overshadowing negative perceptions and criticisms related to human rights abuses, corruption, or other unethical behaviors.” (Boykoff, 2022). In both democratic and authoritarian political environments, sportswashing can occur. It is a social relationship that involves a both local and foreign public.

Countries use sportswashing in two different ways. Some see it as a way to cover up negative actions with the appeal of sports. For example, hosting Formula 1 races to attract tourism and provide visitors a positive image of the country, while forcing them to ignore the negative practices that commonly take place. Other countries view sportswashing as a link between sports and political strategies, like owning sports teams to impact foreign policy. This is often seen in Middle Eastern countries sponsoring European sports teams. These types of deals help build stronger relationships between the countries that appear to be “non-political”. It is particularly favorable for Middle Eastern countries, who are sometimes isolated from business deals due to their questionable reputation. Both of these perspectives regarding sportswashing are just as concerning and tend to promote the abuse of power by authoritarian governments.

The concern provoked by sportswashing comes from evidence of leaked documents, such as the FIFA Files, Football Leaks, and the Pandora Papers (Ganji, 2023). These documents reveal how the sports world, primarily soccer, has helped certain governments hide their finances within the sport’s commercial activities. The governments taking part in this are usually emerging powers, not world leading ones. The most prominent examples include: Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates. Investigations have also revealed that sportswashing has often been used for money laundering. Therefore, concerns regarding this strategy are big and justifiable (Ibidem).

Following this analysis of the concept of sportswashing, it is now necessary to go back to its origins to fully comprehend it. In Ancient Greece, sports were used as propaganda and to show off wealth and power. Part of the reason the Olympic Games were held was for the host city to show off their infrastructure, and their athletes, as well as to distract the public from questionable political situations. “In 416 BCE, amid a war between Athens and Sparta, Athens entered numerous teams into the chariot race, where they thrived. Their success distracted attention from the fact that Athens was being beaten in the war, affording the impression that everything was going well” (Golden, 2008). In the past century, this technique has continued to grow and develop. It was seen in 1934 in Italy when Benito Mussolini's fascist regime held the FIFA World Cup. Italy’s victory in the tournament proved, to Western countries, the strength of the country. This was followed by Germany’s Summer Olympics, held during the Nazi regime in 1936 (Ganji, 2023). This was used as an opportunity for Adolf Hitler to divert attention from his political plans and prove dominance over other European countries. Finally, one of the most important events in the development of the sportswashing technique was the 1978 FIFA World Cup in Argentina. (Ibidem) This event was used as a distraction from the military intervention that was taking place in the country and worked out exactly as the government intended.

As years went by, many sports events were held for political purposes. However, the term sportswashing didn’t exist until 2015, when Azerbaijan was set to host the European Olympic Games. For Azerbaijan, the years leading to this event were dedicated to preparation and planning. During this process, concerns began to rise regarding the practices being used by their autocratic regime (Ganji, 2023). Human rights were being violated on a daily, and anyone who tried to speak up would be imprisoned. This included hundreds of human rights activists, journalists, and dissidents. As the games approached, the concern for the Aliyev regime grew. Several human rights organizations got together to intervene in the matter. They began a campaign called “Sport for Right”, which exposed the truth behind Azerbaijan’s practices and their true purpose for hosting the European Games. They concluded that Azerbaijan was taking advantage of this hosting opportunity to hide its human rights violations and illegal practices. This is when the term sportswashing was born (Ibidem).  Since 2015, Sport for Rights has been dedicated to calling out abusive governments and their use of sports events.

The essence and challenge of sportswashing lie in recognizing when it happens. Numerous efforts have been made to set a measurable standard to identify the practice, however they are not always accurate. The most common approach to identifying sportswashing involves pointing out a well-known sporting event, connecting it to an authoritarian regime, and then listing the regime's violations of norms. This method assumes that such regimes inherently have bad reputations that they aim to cleanse through the visibility sports provide. However, these assumptions are not always accurate (Ganji, 2023). It is necessary to find a way of identifying the practice because the implications of it happening silently and secretly are enormous. Sportswashing creates an information environment where people are essentially encouraged to ignore wrongdoing.

In the past few years, Saudi Arabia has used sports to reach its goals for their Vision 2030 agenda. Prince Mohammed bin Salman intends to diversify the Kingdom’s economy to lead them away from their oil dependency (Sam, 2023). They also intend to modernize the Kingdom’s image and portray a more advanced industry. Creating a sports area is important for creating a new image and helping the economy. Their Vision 2030 marks a significant step towards reducing their oil dependent economy. It will do this through investment in various industries but have recently realized the big role that sports can play in this. Through the creation of new sports infrastructure, the hosting of global events and the investment in international sports, Saudi Arabia aims to promote tourism, job creation and bring in international investment (Ibidem).  This has been shown to work in events such as the Formula 1 races and boxing matches.

The following image shows the three main pillars of Saudi’s Vision 2030. The first one, “a vibrant society” aims to enhance the quality of life for citizens by improving sectors such as health, entertainment, and education. The second pillar, the one we are focusing on throughout this paper, “a thriving economy”, focuses on diversifying the economy beyond oil. The revenue generated from oil has played an important role in financing the Saudi Arabian government’s activities. According to the KPMG Saudi Budget Report of 2019, oil revenues accounted for approximately 68% of total government revenue in 2019 (KPMG, 2018). This strong dependency on the oil revenue has made the Saudi economy vulnerable to the variations in worldwide oil prices (Abhishek, 2023). The third pillar, “an ambitious Nation”, seeks to promote Saudi Arabia’s culture while enhancing the Nation’s economy through domestic knowledge and local efforts. They intend to work towards sustainability and clean energies.

The Ministry of Sport in Saudi Arabia, previously known as the General Sports Authority, was established in 1974 (Nugali, 2019). Over the years, it has undergone significant structural transformations, with changes in leadership and strategy. Saudi Arabia has spent over US$1.5 billion on sports activities since the rise of Mohammed bin Salman (MBS) between 2016 and 2021 (Killingstad, 2022).

Sports are progressively becoming an essential pillar in Saudi Arabia's strategy of economic diversification and image rebranding. Some prime examples include the Roshn Saudi Professional Football League and the global sports stars, Newcastle United acquisition and LIV Golf and its merger with the PGA.

Saudi Arabia’s investment in each sport is meticulously analyzed and strategically selected. Each sport they choose has particular benefits for the Kingdom. The following graph displays the amount of money invested from 2014 to 2023, broken down by sport.

Source: Statista

The data illustrates that motorsports receive the highest investment, with $664 million, followed by wrestling at $500 million, soccer at $169 million, and boxing at $107 million, with other sports receiving significantly less. The big investment in motorsports reflects the global popularity and economic impact of events like Formula 1, which attract international visitors and significant media coverage, contributing to economic growth and tourism. Motorsports events often require big and expensive infrastructure, which leads to high costs but with great benefits in return. Wrestling's high investment might be surprising initially but can be explained by the  promotion of wrestling events, such as those organized by World Wrestling Entertainment (WWE), which have been popular and lucrative. These events are part of a broader cultural and entertainment shift within Saudi Arabia, reflecting its Vision 2030 goals to diversify the economy and increase cultural plurality. Investments in soccer are also high due to the sport's massive global following and its potential to enhance national prestige and unity. Soccer has a broad audience that can significantly increase local engagement with sports and boost international tourism. Boxing, horse racing, and other sports with smaller investments still play essential roles in diversifying sports offerings and attracting niche audiences, contributing to the broader goal of establishing Saudi Arabia as a sports destination. (Zidane, Panja, 2023).

However, evidence points to the fact that Saudi Arabia’s investment in sports is primarily a political strategy. In addition to economic diversification, sportswashing operates as a strategic tool to overshadow negative aspects of Saudi Arabia's domestic and international policies. The Kingdom intends to distract from human rights critiques and political controversies by aligning with pro athletes and hosting mega-events (Zidane, Panja, 2023).

Selecting sports as a tool to enhance their international image is a very analyzed strategy. All through history, sports have been shown to emotionally attach audiences. They are an effective method to move masses or lead them into feeling certain ways. Authoritarian countries such as Saudi Arabia and Qatar have leaned into sports as a way of controlling masses while attempting to hide their illegal practices (Ganji, 2023).

To better understand why Saudi Arabia utilizes sports to shape public opinion, it is important to examine the significant impact that sports have on audiences. According to a research done and published by Harvard graduate Sarath Ganji, sports move the public via three different techniques: distracting the audience from what they might otherwise see, what they might hear and what they might feel (Ibidem).

First, sports have a way of overshadowing negative news by presenting positive ones. News outlets can provide coverage influenced on what they decide the narrative to be. This technique of cherry-picking news serves as a detour, publishing sports stories on the front lines and reducing the possibility of negative stories to be seen by the public. As a result, this shifts the audience’s focus away from what they might have otherwise seen (Ibidem).

Sports also have the ability to shift negative opinions by proposing new and different perspectives. They use high-profile athletes, team owners, and influencers to divert the audience's attention to what they want them to see. These actors become key players in the manipulation of the masses. They help shift the focus from potential critique that the public may hear. For example, Lionel Messi has a lucrative deal to promote tourism for the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, which is reported to earn him up to $25 million over three years  (Zidane, Panja, 2023). Part of the agreement includes a clause that prohibits Messi from saying anything that might tarnish Saudi Arabia's image. His activities as part of this deal have included a variety of promotional efforts, such as sharing posts on social media with his millions of followers and making visits to the country, for which he receives substantial payments. For instance, he reportedly gets $2 million for each promotional post he shares on Instagram and for each visit he makes to Saudi Arabia (Ibidem).

Finally, sports can reduce negative perceptions by provoking different emotions in the public. A lot like religion, sports are founded in human culture and emotion. A lot of what attracts the public is the enthusiasm and emotion that is caused in stadiums and competitions. This feeling creates a profound emotional response that maintains the audience attached and can overshadow negative feelings. By engaging the fans in this way, sports are able to manipulate the crowd’s feelings, it distracts them from negative emotions or critique they might have. This technique has been shown effective during World Cups, where fans ignore the human rights abuses that took place for the organization of the event and rather immerse themselves in the emotion of the matches. “Taken together, these forms of sportswashing yield an information environment in which, to put it starkly, people are made to see no evil, hear no evil, and feel no evil” (Ganji, 2023).

Saudi Arabia does not only manipulate the audience, but also the athletes, persuading them into making deals with them through big monetary offers. Examples of this include Cristiano Ronaldo’s transfer to Al Nassr and Neymar’s transfer to Al Hilal FC. One of the most recent cases when this happened was Saudi Arabia launch of their own professional golf league named LIV Golf. “LIV” in LIV Golf does not stand for an acronym, rather, it represents the Roman numeral for 54, which is important in golf because it symbolizes a perfect score. The emergence of LIV Golf created a big disruption within professional golf, attracting top players from the PGA Tour and opening debate and controversy over the influence of Saudi money in sport (Killingstad, 2022).

The Saudis achieved a merger between the PGA Tour and LIV Golf through strategic investments from their Public Investment Fund (PIF). By offering significant financial incentives, including massive appearance fees and prize money, LIV Golf attracted top golfers, creating a competitive alternative to the PGA Tour. This move pressured the PGA Tour to respond, as it risked losing star players and sponsorships. This is a clear example of how Saudi Arabian investments help them gain influence in the international arena (Sam, 2013).

In order For Saudi Arabia to have more control over their sports strategy, they have inserted their power into the media. The interaction between media freedom and sports, especially in the context of sportswashing, creates a complicated situation. Fans of sports teams often feel deeply connected to them, leading to strong emotional reactions when any negative news about the clubs, their management, or owners emerges (Higgins, 2022). Sportswashing takes advantage of media exposure to portray owners in a positive light while painting journalists who criticize them negatively. As authoritarian governments aim to enhance their global reputation through the acquisition of sports teams or hosting major events, the gap between the media and these sports clubs widens, making it harder for journalists to provide genuine criticism. This typically leads to significant issues being overlooked in favor of more trivial sports coverage (Ibidem).

Saudi Arabia's approach to sports extends to shaping international media narratives. By focusing media attention on athletic excellence and cultural events, Saudi Arabia manages to create a diversion from critical discussions about its governance and human rights record. The country's increased visibility in the sports world promotes its preferred narrative while minimizing critical voices.

The most relevant case regarding media manipulation in Saudi Arabia is the case of reporter Jamal Khashoggi. He was a Saudi Arabian journalist known for his critical perspectives on Saudi Arabia's government policies. He wrote for several major newspapers, including the Washington Post, and his work often published issues of political freedom, human rights, and reform within Saudi Arabia. This kind of information is not well received in the Kingdom. Khashoggi went into self-imposed exile in the United States in 2017 due to the rising danger and threats he faced in his country (Fauntleroy, 2021).

Tragically, his life ended on October 2, 2018, when he was murdered inside the Saudi consulate in Istanbul, Turkey. This event brought international concern and placed Saudi Arabia on the spotlight for its human rights violations. The extreme measures taken by the government rose to the spotlight. After investigation by the U.S intelligence, they concluded that the assassination had been approved by the government and signed by Prince Mohammad bin Salman. This created tension in the relationships between Saudi and many Western countries (Ibidem).

As a result of the tensions, social media in Saudi Arabia rose in response. American reporter Collie Fauntleroy reported on the issue, stating the following:

 Twitter users surged to the surface of the conflict in support of Mohammad bin Salman, just prior to the report’s release. Marc Owen Jones, a disinformation researcher based in Qatar, identified over 600 Twitter accounts tweeting the hashtag #thepeopleofthekingdomsupportthecrownprince. Alarmingly, he also found that many of these posts were not made legitimately; in fact, they were made by bots operating under inauthentic accounts (Fauntleroy, 2021).

This case raised important questions on the lack of freedom of expression and adequate media coverage in Saudi Arabia. It drew international attention, provoked a lot of criticism and concern.

To continue taking control of the media and increasing their influence, Saudi Arabia then purchased broadcast rights of major sports channels in Europe. The agreements include partnerships with European broadcasters, such as DAZN, Canal+, Sport TV, Marca.com, La 7, and Cosmote, covering relevant markets across the continent. The deal made with DAZN spans multiple territories, including Austria, Belgium, Canada, Germany, the UK, France, Portugal, Italy, Spain, and Greece (IMG, 2023). The objective of this deal was for the Saudi Pro League (soccer) to be watched by fans worldwide. They intend to increase their exposure and obtain international validation from sport enthusiasts. It is a strategic way for Saudi culture and influence to be infiltrated in Europe. It is clear that media manipulation is an integral part of the project to manipulate the masses and clean Saudi Arabia’s international image (Ibidem).

Furthermore, it is important to note that Saudi Arabians do not get away with sportswashing on their own, it takes two to make a deal. Democratic countries that negotiate with authoritarian governments are just as responsible for the consequences of sportswashing. By agreeing to make deals with authoritarian countries, democracies enable them to reach international engagement and distract from the harsh reality that their country represents (Grix, Dinsmore & Brannagan, 2023).

Both Western and Middle Eastern countries benefit from negotiating with each other. On the one hand, democracies seek economic gain and investment, while on the other, authoritarian regimes want social acceptance and cultural prestige. By working with one another, they exchange these factors  (Ibidem).

The following diagram illustrates this relationship. At the center of the circles are the countries with high cultural prestige and power, these countries include France, the United Kingdom and Germany. These countries can transfer their prestige to countries on the periphery, which wish to improve their image through their involvement in large cultural and sport events. Their relation in showed as a cycle with different phases. First, in “Wave 1”, countries with strong economic capital but lacking in cultural status invest in sports platforms. This starting investment is met with significant media criticism. Further on, in “Wave 2”, these investments continue to grow, but the debate on the morality of the deals continues. Eventually, in “Wave 3”, the investments become normalized, the initial criticism dilutes and the economic benefits of the arrangement grow. As the normalization of the events happen, the global image of the investing countries begins to improve and their soft power increases. Both sides of the equation have now reached their goal, whether it was to gain economic power or cultural prestige (Ibidem).

After this research, it has become evident that sportswashing has become a growing global problem. Sports are used by nations to boost their image or deflect criticism of human rights abuses. Saudi Arabia serves as an example of how authoritarian regimes use sports to promote their own agendas. Saudi Arabia's tactics and the justifications for these actions were investigated throughout this study.

It is clear that a mix of political, economic, and social goals form the basis of Saudi Arabia's purpose. Due to its reliance on oil earnings, the Kingdom has had to diversify its economy in order to maintain long-term financial stability. In order to draw in tourists, foreign investment, and new revenue streams, Saudi Arabia aims to become a worldwide sports ambassador. Aside from this, the Kingdom also wishes to project an image that will overshadow its poor human rights record, therefore strengthening its soft power. Saudi Arabia has frequently been criticized for its repressive laws, which include restrictions on free speech, mistreatment of women, and the use of the death sentence. Sportswashing has been proven to be an effective method to divert attention away from these problems. Through the use of sports, Saudi Arabia wants to gain the reputation of a nation that engages with the international community and welcomes global culture.

Saudi Arabia’s sportswashing determination, methodical approach and techniques are presented throughout this study. Hosting sporting events, such as boxing matches, Formula 1 races and professional golf tournaments, boosts the Kingdom's international profile by bringing in spectators and media attention. Saudi is able to take on these hosting possibilities because of its abundant financial resources. Saudi Arabia uses its resources to get sports teams and organizations to collaborate with them by providing economic incentives. The issue is that when choosing locations for athletic events, this financial influence occasionally leads to the prioritization of economic gains over human rights issues.

As explained throughout the paper, Saudi Arabia's strategy consists of investing in the construction of first-rate sports facilities in addition to hosting tournaments. The Kingdom's dedication to sports is demonstrated by the building of stadiums, racetracks, and training facilities. The goal of this infrastructural focus is to create a local sporting community in addition to drawing in international events. Saudi Arabia wants to come out as a modern, progressive country, therefore they portray themselves as sports enthusiasts.

Another important tactic is media manipulation. The monarchy uses strategies to shape public narrative around its athletic accomplishments. In order to highlight achievements rather than human rights issues, Saudi manipulates the media narrative. Also, It has been noted that using the influence of well known athletes has been effective to promote Saudi’s political agenda. Athletes are drawn into the government's campaign by participating in competitions and representing teams connected to the monarchy. By taking part in these sporting events, athletes are inherently supporting the sportswashing efforts by Saudi Arabia.

Concern has grown about how the international community may evolve in the future, resulting from the kingdom's attempts to project a positive image of itself. The normalization of this behavior is one of the primary worries. If Saudi Arabia keeps making significant sports investments, there is a chance that people will grow accustomed to sportswashing tactics. Over time, the initial resistance to these activities may lessen and eventually become socially acceptable. Normalization of this kind could encourage other regimes to follow Saudi Arabia's lead without fear of consequences. Sportswashing has the potential to seriously weaken the ability of the international community to hold those who violate human rights accountable. The fact that athletes and sports organizations may become less credible as the public becomes aware of their tactics is another big concern. Sports fans may lose faith in athletic events if they witness governments getting involved in sports-related matters. Supporters may become less interested in important sports events due to their suspicion that they could be used as political instruments.

It is expected that the way in which sportswashing is handled may change in the future. Saudi Arabia is expected to increase its sports investment by 2034 (Hadchity, 2023). With better international relations and infrastructure, Saudi Arabia could be able to host major athletic events like the Olympic Games and World Cup. As a result of this sector's expansion, Saudi Arabia may continue hosting sporting events, which could normalize the relationship between political interests and sports. It is anticipated that social media and technology could have an impact on sportswashing in the future, potentially limiting the government's use of these strategies. As media channels and internet accessibility increase, countries may find it more difficult to maintain control over the narrative around their athletic events. Inconsistencies could be revealed by independent journalists, campaigners, and even athletes using media channels. If public consciousness and opposition result in a reduction of the effects of sportswashing, Saudi Arabia may have to consider new ways to strengthen its soft power standing. Their international relationships may face difficulties as a result of this change.

In spite of the challenges, there are indications of hope to decrease the influence of sportswashing. An increasing number of people are rebelling against these strategies. The work of groups like Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International to expose cases of sportswashing is becoming more popular. A growing number of independent journalists and even athletes are speaking out against sportswashing and in favor of moral conduct in sports. These organizations might demand rules for choosing event hosts that prioritize ethical and human rights records.

The research that took place for this paper is relevant to both academics and policy-makers. By continuing with this study and conducting further research in this area, researchers can gain a better understanding of the intersections between sports and disciplines such as science, economics, and human rights studies. The effects in international perception and the economy can be investigated further. The study's conclusions can also help legislators create agreements and guidelines that support transparency in the sports industry. The knowledge from this study could help them establish protocols that guarantee the procedures used in the selection of venues for athletic events are done fairly.  Legislators may also decide to increase media coverage of sporting events and improve financing transparency for sports.

 In conclusion, to overcome the problems caused by sportswashing, a change in the way the international community functions is necessary. Going forward, rather than viewing sports just as forms of enjoyment, we must view them as powerful platforms that may contribute to human rights advocacy and promote constructive social change. By committing to a code of conduct and placing rights above financial gain, we can maintain the principles of fair competition and high athletic achievement. The decision is clear, we can either watch indifferently while sports are abused for the personal gain of a select few or use their influence to build a society that values sportsmanship and promotes fairness and compassion.
